How Bullsburg helps it stick

  • Warm-up guesses. Before each new lesson, your child guesses at 2 questions. Research shows that guessing first, even wrongly, helps people remember more. The same questions come back after the lesson.
  • Spaced review. Questions from earlier lessons come back after 1 day, 3 days, a week, two weeks and a month. A "Review due today" button shows when some are waiting.
  • Practice tests with explanations. Every wrong answer shows the right one and why.
  • Teach it back. After each money lesson, your child can explain the idea in their own words, like teaching a friend, and see which key points they covered. Explaining something is one of the strongest ways to remember it.
  • Short, regular sessions work best. Two 20-minute sessions a week beat one long one. The progress report shows learning time this week.

How Bullsburg keeps kids safe

  • Pretend money only. Coins in the Practice Market have no real value and can't be cashed out.
  • Grown-up check before any purchase. A math question a young child can't easily answer appears before the store checkout opens.
  • Kid-safe ads only. Optional videos, which your child chooses to watch for bonus coins, are set to general-audience content with no personalized ads and no advertising ID.
  • No chat and no public profiles. Kids can't message other players.
  • AI feedback only for teens 13 and up. A neutral question asks for the birth year once. Teens 13 and older can get written feedback on their Teach it back answers from an AI (Claude, made by Anthropic). Their text is checked for personal info first, isn’t stored by Bullsburg and isn’t used to train AI. Children under 13 never use the AI; they get the built-in checker on the device. In school accounts, the AI stays off unless the teacher turns it on.
  • Little data. Progress is saved on the device. School accounts save progress online so teachers can see it; nothing is sold or used for ads.

Players can share an invite code with friends. When a friend uses it, both get pretend coins. Codes contain no personal information. Please remind your child to share theirs only with people they know.

Talking about money together

Try the parent quiz. Five questions, two minutes, on the same ideas your child is learning. Then ask your child to explain the one you missed.

Family challenges. The progress report and the "Send a note home" message include a short family challenge that matches what your child is learning, such as reading a pay stub together or planning a week of meals on a budget. Each takes about 15 minutes, and you never need to share private numbers.

  • Ask what company they would "buy" in the game, and why.
  • When their pretend portfolio drops, talk about patience and not panicking.
  • Explain that options are risky for real money, and why the game practices them with pretend money first.
  • In the U.S., you must be 18 to open your own brokerage account. Kids can invest through a grown-up's custodial account.
